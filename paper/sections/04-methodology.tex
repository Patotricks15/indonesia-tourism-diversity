\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Relevance model}
A shrunk-cosine item-item co-visitation matrix $S^{\text{cf}}$ is built from
the binary training interaction matrix $X$. A user's relevance score over
every destination is $\hat{r}_u = \sum_{i \in H_u} S^{\text{cf}}_i$, where
$H_u$ is the user's training-time positive set. The top-$P$ scoring unseen
destinations form the candidate pool re-ranked below.

\subsection{Content similarity}
A separate, content-only similarity matrix $S^{\text{ct}}$ is built from
one-hot (category, city) descriptors, cosine-normalised, with city
down-weighted by $0.5$ relative to category.

\subsection{Maximal Marginal Relevance}
MMR selects items greedily. Given already-selected set $Y$, the next item
maximises

\begin{equation}
  \lambda \, \hat{r}(d) - (1-\lambda) \max_{d' \in Y} S^{\text{ct}}(d, d'),
  \label{eq:mmr}
\end{equation}

where $\lambda \in [0,1]$ trades relevance for diversity; $\lambda=1$
recovers plain relevance ranking.

\subsection{Determinantal Point Process}
A DPP models a whole subset's probability as proportional to
$\det(L_S)$, where $L_S$ is the kernel restricted to subset $S$. We use the
quality-diversity decomposition

\begin{equation}
  L_{ij} = \hat{r}(i)\, \hat{r}(j)\, S^{\text{ct}}(i, j), \quad L_{ii} = \hat{r}(i)^2,
  \label{eq:dpp-kernel}
\end{equation}

so that a large diagonal rewards relevance and small off-diagonal entries
reward dissimilarity. Exact MAP inference over subsets of size $k$ is
NP-hard; we use the fast greedy approximation of Chen, Zhang and Zhou
\cite{chen2018fastgreedy}, which incrementally updates a Cholesky-like
factor $c_i$ and residual $d_i^2$ in $O(k^2 n)$ instead of recomputing a
determinant from scratch at every step.
