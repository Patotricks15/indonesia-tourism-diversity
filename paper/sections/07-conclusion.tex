\section{Limitations and conclusion}
MMR's trade-off is controlled by a single scalar $\lambda$ that is not
learned from data; a production system would tune it against a business
objective (e.g. session-level engagement) rather than a fixed offline
split. The DPP kernel's quality-diversity decomposition assumes the content
similarity matrix is the only axis worth diversifying along, ignoring
price or geographic proximity. Both re-rankers also inherit any popularity
bias already present in the underlying collaborative relevance score, since
neither alters \emph{which} items are relevant, only their relative order.
Overall, MMR and DPP are both effective, non-neural, deterministic
mechanisms for trading a measured amount of relevance for a measured gain
in list diversity.
